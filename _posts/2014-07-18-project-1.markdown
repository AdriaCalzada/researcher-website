description: Synthesis gas (syngas), a mixture of hydrogen (H<sub>2</sub>) and carbon monoxide (CO), is key in a wide range of critical industrial processes, from Fischer-Tropsch hydrocarbon synthesis to the water gas shift reaction, or methanol synthesis. The performance and selectivity are highly dependent on the H<sub>2</sub>:CO ratio, and so mechanisms are needed to control the syngas mixture, where membranes are currently one of the most studied, primarily due to their ease of use and low cost. The present study proposes grazynes, two-dimensional (2D) carbon-based materials that combine graphene stripes with sp<sup>2</sup>-hybridized C atoms linked together through acetylenic linkages with sp C atoms, as potential candidates to separate and modulate H<sub>2</sub> from CO in syngas streams by differential permeability. A combination of density functional theory (DFT) calculations and molecular dynamics (MD) simulations on proper grazyne models with nano-engineered pores allowed studying the viability in a holistic fashion, regarding thermodynamic, kinetic, and dynamic aspects, as well as the possible pore obturation. The results show that grazynes physisorb both H<sub>2</sub> and CO, with diffusion rates exceeding 1 s<sup>−1</sup> across the entire temperature range of 100-500 K. CO exhibits physisorption with E<sub>ads</sub> values of ca. -0.16 to -0.21 eV, stronger than H<sub>2</sub>, showing values from ca. -0.07 to -0.09 eV, which bias the observed preferential H<sub>2</sub> diffusion. Kinetic phase diagrams (KPDs) reveal no pore obturation in normal pressure and temperature conditions. Finally, rate estimates show a very high selectivity for H<sub>2</sub> over CO, particularly at temperatures below 300 K, while MD simulations reveal that H<sub>2</sub> can constitute up to 99% of the filtered gas when using [1],[2]{(0,0),2}-grazyne from a H<sub>2</sub>:CO 3:1 mixture, with filtering being tunable by the grazyne, syngas composition, pressure and temperature, but not very affected by the presence of carbon dioxide (CO<sub>2</sub>) or methane (CH<sub>4</sub>) impurities, thus posing grazynes with nano-engineered pores as appealing materials for syngas composition control under realistic working conditions.
